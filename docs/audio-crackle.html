Bug report

The crackling voice.

When T.R.I.S.T.A.N. spoke on the Mac speakers there was a crackle: at first all through the line, then at its start and its end, and later in the demo recordings. It had six separate causes. This page records each one, how it was found and what fixed it.

Summary

  • Symptom. “Crackling” while it speaks, on the Mac speakers, with nothing else playing. Later: a crack at the end of a line, a crack at the start sometimes, and cracking in the demo recordings.
  • Not one bug. Six causes stacked up: a low-quality voice, the speakers switching off after a line, the speakers waking up late, a sound effect landing on top of the voice, and the voice being too loud, so its peaks clipped.
  • Status. All six are fixed in code, and the measurements on the new recordings are clean. Not proven by ear: I could not listen, and the first automatic checks missed a real problem, so the final judgement belongs to a person listening.

The six causes

3Crack at the start, sometimes. The output was only opened on the first spoken line, so the speakers were still waking up. It also stopped silently after an output-device change.It followed the fix for cause 2 and appeared only sometimes.The output opens at launch and restarts when the audio device changes.
4Effects on top of the voice. The mission-start, approval and mission-complete sounds played at the same moment as the spoken line.A recording showed the effect and the voice summing in the first seconds.A spoken line waits for a playing effect (up to 1.3 s). An effect that lands during speech is made quieter in its own samples. The player’s volume is never changed mid-sound, because that steps the level and clicks.
5The voice itself clipped. At full volume the voice peaked above full scale in the mix, and clipped peaks are heard as crackle.First recording with sound: peak 1.13, with 47 samples above 1.0. The offline render had looked fine (peak 0.73), which hid it.The speech level is 0.6. In the latest recordings the loudest peak is about −10 dB, with no clipping.
6The kids voice. Kids mode used the child-like Junior voice, a compact voice, with its pitch raised 30%. Stretching a basic voice that far makes it squeaky and rough.Offline render of the kids lines: Junior at +30% scored 0.73 on the crackle measure, above the 0.48 of a clean reference. Samantha at +12% scored 0.00, and her Enhanced version also 0.00.Kids mode now speaks as Samantha, a little brighter (+12%) and 5% slower, and uses her Enhanced version when installed (scripts/install-voices.sh en_US). The kids video was re-recorded: peak −11 dB, no clipping.

How it was measured

Because I could not hear the app, every claim was turned into a number.

  • Offline render. AVSpeechSynthesizer.write renders a voice to samples without playing it. That showed the voice quality (cause 1) and that the waveform ends cleanly (cause 2).
  • Recording the app’s own sound. scripts/record-window.swift uses ScreenCaptureKit to film the T.R.I.S.T.A.N. window and capture only that app’s audio in the same file, so the demo videos finally have sound. This is what exposed causes 4 and 5.
  • Checks on the recording. Peak level and the number of samples at or above full scale; clicks found by looking for spikes in the second difference of the signal; silent gaps; and a “crackle per voiced second” score compared with a clean render. The recorder also counts timing faults between audio buffers, to rule out the capture itself.

The level seems to scale roughly with the square of the setting, which is why 0.7 cut the peak by more than half. The crackle score was 0.48 per voiced second for a clean offline render and 0.00 for both final videos. The recorder found 0 timing faults in about 1,800 audio buffers.

What was ruled out

  • Music ducking. It still crackled with nothing playing. A small ramp for the volume change was added anyway, and it is harmless.
  • Bluetooth. It happened on the Mac speakers.
  • Speech rate and pitch. Changing them made no meaningful difference.
  • Clipping in the offline render. There was none (peak 0.73). It only appeared in the live mix, so a clean-looking render does not prove clean playback.
  • The recording tool. Zero timing faults between buffers, and the recording is separate from what the speakers receive.
  • The silent gaps in recordings. A 128 ms gap that looked like a dropout is the designed pause between an effect and the voice.

What is not proven

  • All measurements are on the audio the app hands to the system. A fault between the system and the speakers would not show up in them.
  • The first detector missed the clipping, because it looked for clicks and the problem was a flat-topped peak. A listener reported cracking that these metrics could not show at one point, so listening is still the deciding test.
  • The silent output loop uses a little power while the app runs, and a speech line can start up to about a second later after a sound effect. Both are deliberate trade-offs for a cleaner sound.

How to check it yourself

  1. Run scripts/install-voices.sh and download Daniel (Enhanced), then restart the app.
  2. Start a mission and listen to the start, the approval request and the end, on the Mac speakers.
  3. Record the demo with scripts/record-demo.sh and open the file: it contains the app’s own sound.

The changes

In order, on GitHub: 722a861 (voice hint, install script, gentler cut-off), 36d65ee (silent output loop), 869cab4 (open at launch), e2c1966 (no overlap with effects), a6da593 (voice level), c73b17e (quality first: quieter voice, no volume steps), 4ba53ae (kids voice) and adb05af (kids video with the Enhanced voice).
